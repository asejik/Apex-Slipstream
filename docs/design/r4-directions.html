R4 · Three design directions

Each direction shows the same three screens on a landscape phone (844 × 390): the home hub, the race HUD and the race results. Pick one, or mix parts (for example, one direction's HUD with another's menus).

All three share the same fixes: the stack of 8 buttons at the top right goes into a pause menu behind one pause button (U-08, U-12); the HUD keeps only what a racer reads at speed; position, lap time and gap sit in fixed corners.

Feel references only. No logos, fonts, colours or art are copied from F1 or eFootball.

A · Broadcast

Feel reference: the TV graphics of an F1 race. Flat, sharp-edged panels, condensed capitals, a timing tower.

  • Reads instantly: white on near-black, no glow, numbers in fixed-width figures so they don't jitter.
  • Timing tower on the left shows the cars around you with their gaps, like a broadcast.
  • One red accent for "act here" (start, your row), green for gains, purple for fastest lap.
  • Cheapest to draw on a phone: no blur or glow effects.

B · Matchday

Feel reference: the eFootball menus. A glossy tile hub, rounded cards, bright colour, rewards shown up front.

  • Menus feel like a game hub: one big "play next" tile, smaller tiles for the other modes, tabs along the top.
  • Strong on rewards (XP, points, unlocks), which suits R9.
  • HUD is rounder and softer, with a dial speedometer in the middle; busier than A at speed.
  • Uses a blur behind HUD panels, which costs more on low-end phones (can be switched off).

C · Telemetry Mk II

An evolution of today's look: dark carbon, electric cyan, cut corners, a faint grid.

  • Least change from what you have now; the existing colour tokens mostly stay.
  • Tidier than today: fewer panels, less glow, one menu list instead of floating buttons.
  • Closer to sci-fi racing than to F1 or eFootball, so it moves least towards the references you gave.
